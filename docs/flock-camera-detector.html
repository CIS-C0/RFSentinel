Flock & plate-reader cameras

RF Sentinel finds Flock and other plate-reader cameras in two layers: radio signatures for cameras that broadcast, and an offline map of every plate reader, speed and red-light camera volunteers have mapped in OpenStreetMap, for the ones that don't.

Layer 2: the known-camera map

  • Automatic: for the area on screen, cameras tagged surveillance:type=ALPR plus fixed speed and red-light cameras are fetched once from the Overpass API and kept offline, refreshed weekly.
  • Download nearby cameras: every plate reader within the radius set in Settings or the setup wizard (10-200 km, 100 by default).
  • Download Flock: every plate reader mapped in the US and Canada, ~18 MB, about 143,000 cameras, refreshed weekly on WiFi. Uses DeFlock's hourly snapshot, then adds cameras it leaves out (other tagging, Flock cameras mapped as ordinary cameras) straight from OpenStreetMap.

While scanning, you're warned once per camera per 30 minutes when one is within about 20 s of travel (150 m minimum, 600 m maximum) and two GPS fixes show you getting closer. Speed cameras warn about 30 s ahead with the mapped limit. Where OpenStreetMap has the camera's direction, a cone in its colour shows which way it faces when you zoom in; while you're moving, a camera facing across your road (watching a cross street) doesn't warn, while one facing you or away (front or rear plates) does. Tap a camera → Ignore alerts to silence it for good, or Set direction when its direction isn't mapped (kept on your phone only).

Why the map matters

Most plate readers that aren't Flock (Genetec AutoVu, Motorola Vigilant, Perceptics, Quercus, Tattile) send their data over cellular modems or wired links and broadcast nothing a phone can match. The map layer is the only way a phone can warn about those. If a camera near you is missing, add it to OpenStreetMap, for example through DeFlock.

Questions

How close do I need to be to detect a Flock camera by radio?

Close: Flock units' WiFi and BLE are low-power and often only heard within tens of metres, depending on the install. RF Sentinel's camera map layer warns from further away.

Does the camera download reveal my location?

When RF Sentinel downloads cameras, the Overpass API and DeFlock's CDN see your IP and the area requested (DeFlock: which 20° region), like map tiles. Turn off Show known cameras automatically to fetch only when you tap a download.

Can I export where I've seen Flock cameras?

Yes. RF Sentinel's recorded traces export as GPX, KML, GeoJSON or CSV with the devices heard along them, including flagged-only.

Is there an app that detects Flock cameras?

Yes. RF Sentinel is a free, open-source Android app that detects Flock Safety cameras and Raven sensors from their WiFi and Bluetooth, and warns offline before every plate reader mapped on OpenStreetMap through DeFlock. No root, no account.

Can RF Sentinel tell me if my plate was read or searched?

No. Plate reads and searches stay in the camera company's and police systems, and no app can see them from the road. RF Sentinel tells you where the cameras are and alerts as you approach one, so you know when you pass one. Public-records requests for audit logs are the way to learn about searches.

How do I avoid Flock cameras while driving?

Turn on Avoid plate readers in RF Sentinel's built-in navigation: it plans the route around the known cameras and says how many are left on it. A camera on the only way in or out can't be avoided, and RF Sentinel still warns about 20 seconds before it. See routes that avoid cameras.

Can I get Flock camera alerts on Android Auto?

Yes. RF Sentinel reads plate-reader and camera alerts aloud through the car. See Android Auto for what shows on the car screen and how to set it up.

Can I use an ESP32 Flock detector like Flock You or OUI-Spy with my phone?

Yes. RF Sentinel reads Flock You and OUI-Spy ESP32 boards connected to the phone and merges what they hear with its own detections and alerts. See ESP32 boards.
