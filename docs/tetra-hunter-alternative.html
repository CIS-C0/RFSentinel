Tetra Hunter alternative

Tetra Hunter is a paid Android app that uses two RTL-SDR dongles to detect TETRA radios, the encrypted digital radios emergency services carry in much of Europe. RF Sentinel detects TETRA radios the same way for free, with its full source on GitHub: one dongle to start, a second or third to watch the band nonstop, and police, camera and tracker detection on top.

Hunting TETRA radios with RF Sentinel

  1. Plug an RTL-SDR (RTL-SDR Blog V3 or V4) into the phone with a USB OTG adapter and turn on Detect TETRA radios in Settings → RTL-SDR radio.
  2. For nonstop watching, add a second RTL-SDR through a powered USB hub. It watches 380-385 MHz while the first keeps sweeping; a third splits the band with it (380-382.4 and 382.6-385 MHz). The status line shows what each one watches.
  3. Antenna: a vertical whip about 19.5 cm long (a quarter wave), on the roof if you can. Two dongles share one antenna through a 2-way splitter.
  4. Turn on TETRA only to put every dongle on the TETRA band, and City mode in towns full of radios.
  5. While a radio is heard, the TETRA bar on the main screen shows how strong it is (Far / Closer / Very close) and the beeps speed up as you get closer.

Every setting is explained in the RTL-SDR guide.

Questions

Is there a free alternative to Tetra Hunter?

Yes. RF Sentinel is free and open source (GPL-3.0): no subscription, no account. With an RTL-SDR dongle it watches the TETRA handset band (380-385 MHz) for the time-slot bursts TETRA radios send, and with a second or third dongle it watches that band nonstop.

How does a TETRA detector work?

TETRA handsets and car radios transmit on 380-385 MHz in short time slots: a burst of about 14 ms every 57 ms. RF Sentinel measures the energy on each 12.5 kHz channel and reports only bursts with that rhythm, so plain carriers and other radios don't count. It doesn't decode anything, and TETRA's encryption isn't touched.

What hardware do I need?

An Android phone with USB OTG and one RTL-SDR dongle (an RTL-SDR Blog V3 or V4, for example) is enough to start. For hunting, add a second dongle on a powered USB hub: it watches the band nonstop while the first keeps sweeping the other bands. Use a vertical antenna about 19.5 cm long, on the roof if you can; two dongles can share it through a 2-way splitter.

Does it work in France or Switzerland?

No, and neither does Tetra Hunter: those countries use TETRAPOL, a different system. TETRA detection works where emergency services use TETRA on 380-385 MHz, which covers most of Europe and many other countries.
