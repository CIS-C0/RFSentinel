RF_SENTINELRF Sentinel - police detector for Android

A free, open-source police detector for Android. It listens to the Bluetooth LE advertisements and WiFi beacons around you and flags police and surveillance equipment: body cameras, Flock and other plate-reader cameras, patrol-vehicle gear, trackers following you, Remote ID drones and camera glasses. It also watches the cell network for fake-tower (IMSI catcher) signs.

RX ONLYNo root · no Termux · no account · no analytics · GPL-3.0 · Android 8.0+ (ARM, 64 and 32-bit)

Features

Live list, radar & map

Every device in range (each leaves 30 s after it was last heard on Bluetooth, 60 s on WiFi, adjustable) with vendor, type, radio, RSSI, rough distance and NEW / ★ / FOLLOWING badges. Flagged devices sort first. Filter chips with live counts, by kind too (vehicles, phones, computers, audio, cameras, routers, hotspots, TVs, smart home, Apple, other), and the list grouped by category. Show the list, the radar and the map one at a time or together, resizable.

Alerts

Sound, vibration pattern per tier (1 / 2 / 3 pulses), spoken announcements through the phone's TTS, optional radar-detector beeps that speed up as a flagged device gets closer, discreet mode, floating threat bubble over Waze or Google Maps with a pop-up saying what was detected. Radar-detector sound style with selectable alert effects and an optional intro.

Navigation

Turn-by-turn directions on the map: search a place (Home, Work, starred and recent places are kept on the phone, and starred ones can be renamed; or long-press the map), pick one of up to three routes, avoid tolls, highways or ferries, and go. A banner shows the next turn, lane arrows and the turn after it; spoken directions play as navigation audio so your music dips under them; it re-routes by itself, takes stops, and finds petrol, charging or food along your route. Your arrival time follows how you really drive. Open the destination in Google Maps or Waze if you prefer. No live traffic. The map goes to its night look after sunset.

Map & traces

A vector map (OpenFreeMap, OpenStreetMap data, or Mapbox if you prefer) with your GPS trace, devices placed where their signal peaked and flagged ones pinpointed from every spot you passed (with the circle they're very likely in), history heatmap and timeline, offline known-camera layer. Save a snapshot of the device list to look at again later. North up, heading up, or a 3D driving view like a navigation app that zooms with your speed, with the default zoom and angle in Settings; a light map and a readable night map with shops, landmarks and one-way arrows. Optional CCTV layer: street, shop and building cameras mapped in OpenStreetMap, with their view cones.

Android Auto

Police alerts on the car screen as messages read aloud (reply "mute"), and an RF Sentinel screen with threats, cameras ahead, recent alerts and Scan / Mute buttons. The full car app with live map and navigation runs on Google's Desktop Head Unit. Details

Auto start in the car

Starts when the phone connects to your car's Bluetooth or Android Auto, stops when you leave. It can also record a trace of every drive by itself.

Sentinel Link

Link RF Sentinel phones near each other over Bluetooth with a 6-digit code: each one shows what the others detect, external hardware included, on its list, radar and map, marked VIA with the phone's name, with full alerts. Encrypted, no internet. Phones far apart can share through Meshtastic LoRa nodes, watchlist matches first (not yet tested on real hardware). Details

Tools

Cell towers (serving and neighbour cells, IDs, channel, signal, history, optional map layer and change alert) and WiFi channel / spectrum analyzers for 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz.

USB WiFi adapters

ALFA AWUS036ACS, AWUS1900, AWUS036ACM, AWUS036H, AWUS036NH / NEH, AWUS036NHA, AWUS036AXML / AXM, TP-Link Archer T3U and other RTL8812BU dongles - 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz on the AC adapters and 6 GHz on the WiFi 6E AWUS036AXML - in receive-only monitor mode over OTG, no root: no scan limit, longer range, devices connected to networks, and the network names devices ask for. The Bluetooth part of a combo adapter, or a USB Bluetooth dongle, adds a second Bluetooth LE scanner. Details

RTL-SDR and HackRF

An RTL-SDR dongle or a HackRF One on OTG notices two-way radios transmitting nearby, TETRA handsets by their time-slot bursts, and optionally cellular modems, from signal strength only: nothing is decoded. It can also receive ADS-B aircraft directly, with no internet, and shows a live radio waterfall of the watched bands (tap a signal to zoom in). The HackRF is used to receive only.

ESP32 boards

OUI-Spy over USB OTG or Bluetooth, GhostESP and Marauder over USB (dual-band on ESP32-C5, or through a Flipper Zero with a BFFB). The board's detections merge into the app. Details

Device details

Evidence, identity, decoded protocol data, signal graph, Locate mode (beeps faster as you get closer, keeps following a tag when it changes its address, and keeps going on the map, where a live read card shows the signal, rough distance and trend), history and the raw advertisement. Give a device your own name or write a note on it: the name shows in the list and on the map, and named or noted devices stay in the history. I saw it adds a device to your watchlist with what you say it is, including its Bluetooth advert pattern or WiFi probe fingerprint, so it's recognised next time even under a new random address. Search every device the app remembers, in range or not, by address, name, vendor, type, your name for it or your note.

Export

CSV, JSON, KML, GPX, GeoJSON. Match history and the watchlist are searchable. Everything stays on the phone until you export or share it.

13 themes and a theme maker

Including DedSec (Watch Dogs 2) and fsociety (Mr. Robot) fan themes with animated terminal headers, Night Drive red HUD, Night Vision, Amber CRT. Make your own: colours, font, banner, header and map marker, and share it as a file.

Privacy

  • Receive-only. The app never transmits, jams, spoofs or connects to the devices it detects.
  • No accounts, no cloud, no analytics. Detections live in a local database excluded from cloud backup.
  • Network use: the map (vector tiles from OpenFreeMap, OpenStreetMap data, or from Mapbox if you pick it in Settings with Mapbox's own usage statistics switched off), camera data (Overpass API, DeFlock CDN), the roads of your area while the Waze option to alert only for reports on your road is on, and, when you search for a destination or start a route, Photon and Nominatim (place search) and Valhalla (routes), only when used. Those servers see your IP and the area or route, never your scans. Two optional sources are off by default: police aircraft (a position rounded to ~1 km goes to the ADS-B feeds adsb.fi / adsb.lol, unless you turn the online feeds off and use only an RTL-SDR's own ADS-B receiver) and Waze reports, either through your own OpenWeb Ninja key or straight from Waze (Waze direct: your IP address and a position rounded to a 1 km grid), at your own risk.
  • Data leaves the phone only when you export or share it.
