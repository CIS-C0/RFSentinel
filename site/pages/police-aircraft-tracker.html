Police helicopter & plane tracker

Police and sheriff helicopters, state patrol planes and federal aircraft broadcast ADS-B like every other aircraft. RF Sentinel matches them against the law-enforcement registry and alerts when one is overhead, from community feeds or from an RTL-SDR on your phone with no internet at all.

What counts as a police aircraft

  • Its ICAO address is in the bundled registry of about 1,400 law-enforcement airframes from the FAA and Transport Canada: police, sheriff, state patrol, federal agencies, RCMP and provincial police.
  • The feed names a police owner, or a government owner (shown as a government aircraft, not always police).
  • Aircraft circling overhead: an unlisted aircraft that keeps orbiting low over the same area. Police and surveillance flights do this, but so do news, training and survey flights: verify.

A strong match within the alert distance (5 km by default) alerts; the rest are listed and logged. Aircraft show on the map with altitude, speed and heading (Tools → Aircraft), and on the Android Auto Threats tab.

Questions

Can my phone tell me when a police helicopter is overhead?

Yes, RF Sentinel does when the helicopter broadcasts ADS-B and is in the registry or circling low. With internet, the community feeds cover it; without, an RTL-SDR on the phone receives ADS-B directly.

Why don't Flightradar24 and similar apps show police aircraft?

Commercial trackers honour blocking requests from aircraft owners, and many law-enforcement aircraft are blocked. The community feeds RF Sentinel uses don't filter them, and an RTL-SDR hears whatever the aircraft broadcasts.

Will it see an aircraft with its transponder off?

No. Without ADS-B, RF Sentinel has nothing to decode. Drones are different: Remote ID drones are covered by the drone detector.

Who is flying over my house right now?

RF Sentinel shows the aircraft around you from their own ADS-B broadcasts, from community feeds or from an RTL-SDR with no internet, and matches them against the law-enforcement registry, so police and government aircraft stand out.
