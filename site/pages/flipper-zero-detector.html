Pocket hacking tools have identifiers of their own, and the attacks they run (Bluetooth pop-up spam, evil twin networks, deauthentication floods) leave patterns a receiver can spot. RF Sentinel's optional Hacking tools category watches for both.

Attacks

  • Evil twin WiFi: the same network name from two different makers with mismatched security (60).
  • Bluetooth spam floods: bursts of new pairing pop-up adverts and fake-tracker floods.
  • WiFi deauthentication attacks (needs a USB WiFi adapter in monitor mode): floods of unprotected deauthentication frames knocking devices off a network, with the tool's pattern where it has one (ESP32 Marauder, also on Flipper WiFi boards, sends only reason code 2).

Off by default: turn on Hacking tools under Settings → detection.

Questions

Can I tell if someone has a Flipper Zero near me?

Yes, while its Bluetooth is on: it advertises a Flipper service UUID, company ID or address block that RF Sentinel matches at 85-90. With Bluetooth off it sends nothing a phone can see.

Can an Android phone detect a WiFi deauth attack?

Not with its own WiFi: Android doesn't hand apps management frames. Plug a supported USB WiFi adapter into the phone and RF Sentinel reads them in monitor mode, no root.

Is there a Bluetooth spam detector for Android?

Yes. RF Sentinel flags Bluetooth pop-up spam floods (bursts of new pairing adverts and fake-tracker floods) and names the Flipper Zero devices it hears, on a non-rooted phone.
