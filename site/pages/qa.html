Detecting police

How do I detect police with an Android phone?

Modern police gear is full of radios that broadcast on their own: body cameras advertise over Bluetooth LE so the officer's phone and holster sensors can find them, in-car routers run WiFi access points, radios and e-ticket printers pair over Bluetooth. Any phone can receive those broadcasts without connecting to anything.

RF Sentinel runs a continuous BLE scan and periodic WiFi scans, and matches every packet against vendor company IDs, service UUIDs, payload tags, network names and IEEE MAC prefixes assigned to law-enforcement equipment makers (Axon/TASER, Motorola Solutions, Digital Ally, Getac, Zebra, Flock Safety and others). When two or more kinds of police-type gear rise and fall in signal together, it flags a possible police vehicle.

Install the APK, tap Start scanning, grant location and Nearby devices. See the police detection guide.

Is there an app that detects police nearby?

Yes: RF Sentinel is a free, open-source Android app that does this passively from Bluetooth LE and WiFi signals, with no root and no subscription. It detects the equipment, not people: a match means a device with a police-equipment signature is in range. Crowd-sourced apps (Waze) rely on users reporting; RF Sentinel relies only on what the hardware itself broadcasts, so it also works where nobody has reported anything.

Can my phone detect a police body camera?

Often, yes. Body cameras use Bluetooth LE to pair with the officer's phone app, holster and light-bar triggers, and many advertise continuously. RF Sentinel matches:

  • the BWCDEVICE ASCII tag in service data, manufacturer data or the raw advert, in either byte order (confidence 90, field-validated);
  • TASER International company ID 0x034D and Axon / TASER service UUIDs 0xFC81, 0xFE6B, 0xFE6C (60 each);
  • Utility Inc. BodyWorn Remote names (80);
  • IEEE MAC prefixes registered to Axon (00:25:DF), Digital Ally, WatchGuard, Getac, Zepcam and others.

Independent matches on the same device are fused, so an Axon camera with the company ID, UUID and prefix lands in the strong tier. Cameras that are switched off, or that only use WiFi to dock, are not visible.

How do I detect Axon body cameras and TASERs?

Axon (formerly TASER International) gear is identifiable from the Bluetooth SIG company ID 0x034D, the 16-bit service UUIDs 0xFC81 (Axon Enterprise), 0xFE6B and 0xFE6C (TASER International), and the IEEE block 00:25:DF. Axon Signal Sidearm holster sensors and Signal units, which start body cameras when a gun is drawn or the light bar turns on, are Bluetooth LE too. RF Sentinel ships all of these rules on by default.

Can it detect a police car or unmarked car?

Indirectly. A single Motorola radio, Zebra printer or Cradlepoint router proves nothing, because civilians use them too. RF Sentinel assigns each device a role (body camera, plate reader, two-way radio, vehicle cellular router, mobile printer, in-car computer, rugged laptop, police camera) and groups devices with at least two different roles when their RSSI moves together (Pearson r ≥ 0.6 over at least 8 aligned seconds), or when both are flat (parked) and appeared within 30 s of each other.

The group is flagged "Possible police vehicle" at 24 + 12 × roles, +10 when the signals move together, capped at 88: two roles stay weak (48), three moving together reach 70. An unmarked car is detected the same way if its gear advertises; there is no "unmarked car" signal as such. Details: patrol-vehicle detection.

Can it detect police radios?

It flags radios that expose Bluetooth or WiFi: Motorola Solutions company ID 0x04EC and UUIDs 0xFD8E / 0xFE04, plus IEEE prefixes of P25 / TETRA radio makers. Those are weak on their own (Motorola radios are also used by security guards, schools and construction crews) and become meaningful as part of a patrol-vehicle group.

It does not receive the voice channels themselves. P25 and TETRA run on VHF, UHF and 700/800 MHz and are usually encrypted; a phone has no receiver for those bands. That would need a software-defined radio, and RF Sentinel never decodes or listens to radio traffic.

Is RF Sentinel a radar detector? Does it detect police radar guns?

No. Police radar and lidar use X, K and Ka-band microwave or infrared laser, which a phone cannot receive. "Police radar" in RF Sentinel's list means the Bluetooth/WiFi signatures of radar makers' in-car units when they advertise. For fixed speed and red-light cameras, RF Sentinel uses the OpenStreetMap camera layer and warns about 30 seconds ahead.

Can it detect e-ticket printers?

Yes, with care. Zebra mobile printers advertise UUIDs 0xFE79 / 0xFD66. With a factory-serial name (^[A-Z0-9]{2}[A-Z]{3}\d{9}$), which fleets rarely rename, the score is 50; any other name stays at 30 because the same printers are everywhere in warehouses and retail. Next to a body camera or radio moving with it, it becomes part of a patrol-vehicle group.

How will I be alerted?

Matches above your alert threshold (default 50, probable) play a sound and vibrate: one pulse for weak, two for probable, three for strong. Optional spoken announcements use the phone's own text-to-speech engine, in full ("Axon body camera nearby") or short ("Body cam", "Police car", "Speed camera, 50"). There is a discreet mode, a floating threat bubble over other apps (green / orange / red with a count), a notification, a Quick Settings tile, a home-screen widget, and heads-up alerts in Android Auto.

Why did it flag a device that is not police?

A match is a signature, not an identification. 00:25:DF means "registered to Axon Enterprise", not "a police officer". Some identifiers are shared with consumer products: Motorola Solutions radios are used by private security, Zebra printers by shops, Meta's company ID by Quest headsets. That is why every rule has a confidence, weak matches are amber and silent by default, and the detail screen shows the exact evidence and source. Long-press a device to whitelist it.

Flock, ALPR, speed and hidden cameras

How do I detect Flock Safety cameras?

Two ways, combined in the app:

  1. Radio signatures. Flock units and their batteries broadcast identifiable signals: WiFi SSIDs starting with Flock- (88), BLE names FS Ext Battery (80), Penguin-<digits> or FS-<hex> (70, or 80 with company ID 0x09C8), and the XUNTONG company ID 0x09C8 alone (45, shared silicon).
  2. The known-camera map. Flock cameras mapped by volunteers in OpenStreetMap (via DeFlock) are downloaded and kept offline. You are warned when one is within about 20 seconds of travel and getting closer, even for cameras that only use cellular and emit nothing a phone can hear.

See the Flock / ALPR guide.

How do I detect license plate readers (ALPR) on Android?

Most non-Flock plate readers (Genetec AutoVu, Motorola / Vigilant, Perceptics, Quercus, Tattile) are wired or on cellular modems and don't broadcast anything a phone can match. RF Sentinel flags them by IEEE prefix when they do expose WiFi or Bluetooth, and covers the rest with the OpenStreetMap layer: every element tagged surveillance:type=ALPR, with manufacturer, operator and direction where mapped. The Download Flock option fetches every mapped plate reader in the US and Canada (~18 MB, about 143,000 cameras, refreshed weekly on WiFi); Download nearby cameras fetches a 10-200 km radius.

Can it detect Flock Raven gunshot / audio sensors?

Yes. Raven sensors advertise 128-bit service UUIDs whose short IDs fall in 0x3100-0x3500, matched at confidence 80 (field capture via all-cameras-are-beacons).

Can it warn me about speed cameras and red-light cameras without Waze?

Yes. Fixed speed and red-light cameras mapped in OpenStreetMap are downloaded automatically for the area you look at and kept offline, refreshed weekly. Speed cameras warn about 30 seconds ahead, with the speed limit when it's mapped. No account, no reporting, no location sent anywhere except the area request to the Overpass API. Coverage depends on how well your area is mapped in OpenStreetMap, and mobile speed traps aren't covered.

Can I silence one specific camera I pass every day?

Yes. Tap the camera on the map and choose Ignore alerts. It stays on the map, faded, and never alerts again. Alert again on the same camera, or Settings, turns it back on. Ignored cameras are kept permanently.

Can I find hidden cameras with my phone?

Only cameras that use WiFi or Bluetooth. RF Sentinel has an optional (off by default) network-camera category: SSIDs starting with ARLO_VMB_ / NTGR_VMB_ (88) and IEEE blocks of Arlo, Blink, Ezviz, Uniview, Amcrest, Wyze, Swann, Night Owl, SkyBell and WUUK (65, WiFi only). Turn on the Network / home security camera category in Settings.

Limits you should know: a camera that joins an existing WiFi network as a client is invisible to an Android WiFi scan (only access points are listed), and wired, SD-card-only or cellular cameras emit nothing usable. A lens-reflection or IR check finds those, not a radio app.

Can it identify hidden WiFi networks?

Yes. Hidden networks still send beacons with information elements. RF Sentinel reads the WPS element (manufacturer, model name and number, device name, primary device type), the Cisco CCX AP name (IE 133), the BSSID's IEEE registrant and vendor IE owners, and links a hidden BSSID to the visible network on the same router (octets 2-5 equal, last octet within 16). Requires Android 11+ for the raw elements.

AirTags and trackers

How do I know if an AirTag is following me on Android?

An AirTag away from its owner switches to a "separated" Find My frame: Apple company ID 0x004C, type 0x12, length 0x19. RF Sentinel matches that frame (confidence 70), keeps tracking the tag through its address rotations, and raises a may be following you alert when it has been with you for at least 10 minutes while you moved at least 800 m (both adjustable: 2-240 minutes). Tap the device and use Locate: it beeps faster as the signal gets stronger.

It reacts to the same frame Android's built-in unknown-tracker alerts use, but shows it live, with history and a signal graph, instead of after hours.

Does it detect Tile, Samsung SmartTag and Google Find Hub trackers?

Yes. Google Find Hub tags via 0xFEAA service data frame 0x41 (separated; frame 0x40 means near the owner and is ignored), Samsung SmartTag via 0xFD5A and Tile via 0xFEED service data (55). All feed the same follow detection.

Can it find a GPS tracker hidden on my car?

Only Bluetooth trackers (AirTag, SmartTag, Tile, Find Hub tags). LTE / GSM GPS trackers report over the cellular network and don't broadcast anything a phone can identify. For those, a physical inspection or a dedicated RF sweep is needed.

My own AirTag keeps showing up. How do I ignore it?

Long-press it and ignore it. The ignore follows the tag when its address changes: it is carried over through the advert fingerprint link, so you don't have to ignore it again after each rotation. To leave every Apple Find My tag out of the Trackers category, turn on Settings → Exclude Apple AirTags (also in Android Auto → More); Google Find Hub, SmartTag and Tile trackers still alert.

Drones and Remote ID

How do I detect drones near me with my phone?

Drones sold since FAA / EU Remote ID rules must broadcast an ASTM F3411 Remote ID signal over Bluetooth (service data 0xFFFA, app code 0x0D) or WiFi (vendor IE OUI FA:0B:BC, type 0x0D). RF Sentinel decodes it (confidence 95), places the drone on the map with heading, altitude and speed, and raises a "drone overhead" alert when it is within 200 m horizontally (once per drone per 5 minutes). Drone makers' own IEEE blocks (DJI, Parrot, Skydio, Autel, Yuneec...) are also matched at 60. See the drone guide.

Can I see where the drone pilot is?

When the drone's Remote ID includes the System message, yes: Remote ID carries the operator (or take-off) position. The detail screen shows it, and you can open both the drone and operator positions in your maps app. Remote ID also carries the serial number and, where used, an operator ID.

Does it detect police drones?

It detects any drone that broadcasts Remote ID, which includes most police drones (DJI, Skydio, Autel) under current rules. It can't tell who operates it unless the operator ID says so. Exempt or modified drones that don't broadcast Remote ID are not detected.

Stingray / IMSI catchers

How do I detect a Stingray or IMSI catcher on Android without root?

Without root, Android exposes the cells your phone sees (network codes, cell IDs, area codes, radio technology, signal) but not ciphering or signalling messages. RF Sentinel reads them every 15 s and looks for the signs a cell-site simulator leaves in that data:

  • serving cell on a test / reserved MCC (001, 002, 999): 85;
  • sudden switch from 4G/5G to 2G while strong LTE is still visible (the visible side of the 2G downgrade EFF's Rayhunter watches for): 70;
  • a known cell number appearing in a different area (cloned identity), a reserved area code (0, 0xFFFE, 0xFFFF) or cell ID 0;
  • a network country code that doesn't match the SIM while not roaming;
  • the location area changing while GPS shows you standing still, a new cell with no neighbours.

A sign must persist across two checks; 2G drops during calls and area codes flipping at coverage borders are ignored. Several signs together score max + 15, capped at 90. See the IMSI catcher guide.

How reliable is fake cell tower detection on a phone?

It is heuristic. Each sign has innocent explanations (network maintenance, rural 2G, carrier misconfiguration), and a well-configured simulator copying a real cell's identity shows few of them. Treat it as a warning to look closer, not proof. The strongest protection is turning off Allow 2G in your SIM settings (Android 12+), and on Android 15+ enabling Cellular security → Network notifications, which warns when a network asks for your SIM identity or turns off encryption. RF Sentinel's Settings links there.

How does this compare with EFF's Rayhunter?

Rayhunter runs on a cheap mobile hotspot with root access to the modem's signalling (QMDL / diag), so it sees identity requests and ciphering changes directly. A normal Android app can't. RF Sentinel implements the parts of that analysis visible through Android's public cell APIs (2G downgrade, identity anomalies) and points to Android 15's own network notifications for the rest. For the strongest detection, use Rayhunter hardware alongside.

Smart glasses

How do I detect Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses nearby?

Ray-Ban Meta glasses advertise over Bluetooth LE. RF Sentinel matches Luxottica's company ID 0x0D53 (70), company ID 0x058E together with the META_RB_GLASS token (72), and Meta's corporate company ID 0x01AB / UUIDs 0xFEB7 / 0xFEB8 (45, because Quest headsets share them). Snap Spectacles (0x03C2), Vuzix (0x060C) and HeyCyan-SDK glasses (full 128-bit UUID) are matched too. See the smart glasses guide.

Can it tell if the glasses are recording?

No. It tells you the glasses are near you and advertising. Recording state is not part of the advertisement.

SØPHIA and other apps

How can I get s0phia ops (SØPHIA) for free?

SØPHIA is a paid product sold by its own developer, and this site doesn't distribute it or any copy of it. If what you want is the same kind of tool for free, RF Sentinel is a free, open-source (GPL-3.0) alternative: passive Bluetooth LE and WiFi scanning for police gear, plate-reader cameras, trackers and drones, installed as a signed APK, no account. Comparison: RF Sentinel vs SØPHIA.

Is there a free alternative to SØPHIA / SophiaOps?

Yes. RF Sentinel covers passive BLE and WiFi recon with a radar view, risk scoring and a timeline, and adds sourced police, ALPR, tracker, Remote ID and smart-glasses signatures, patrol-vehicle grouping, fake cell tower signs, an offline camera map and Android Auto. It costs nothing and the full source is on GitHub. RF Sentinel is independent and not affiliated with SØPHIA or DetecX.

Do I need Termux to scan for police on Android?

Not with RF Sentinel. It is a regular Android app using the public BLE scan and WiFi scan APIs, so there's no Termux, Termux:API, Python or shell setup.

How is it different from RF Party, BLE Radar (MetaRadar) or Flock You?

RF Party (based on Alan Meekins' DEF CON 31 talk) and BLE Radar are general Bluetooth scanners: they list and track devices, with fewer police-specific rules. Flock You and OUI-Spy are ESP32 firmware focused on Flock and watchlist matching. RF Sentinel combines a sourced signature set (each rule cites the IEEE registry, Bluetooth SIG or published research), evidence fusion, patrol-vehicle grouping, WiFi and cell analysis, the OpenStreetMap camera layer and Android Auto in one Android app, and can ingest OUI-Spy and GhostESP boards. No code from those projects is included.

Is there a Flock You app for Android? (Flock You alternative)

Yes: RF Sentinel detects Flock Safety cameras on an Android phone with no extra hardware, and can also read a Flock You / OUI-Spy ESP32 board. Details

Flock You is open-source (MIT) ESP32 firmware focused on one target: Flock Safety cameras. It runs the radio in 2.4 GHz promiscuous mode, so it sees Flock cameras' WiFi probe requests and SoftAPs that a phone can't, plus Flock battery-pack BLE adverts, and beeps or feeds a dashboard.

RF Sentinel is a phone app that covers Flock as one category among many (body cams, patrol gear, trackers, drones, glasses, fake cell towers) and adds the OpenStreetMap / DeFlock camera map for cellular-only cameras. They combine well: plug an ESP32 running OUI-Spy's Flock-You mode into the phone and RF Sentinel shows its Flock detections next to its own. See ESP32 boards.

Which Android app works with OUI-Spy? (OUI-Spy app for Android)

RF Sentinel connects to OUI-Spy boards over USB OTG or Bluetooth and merges their detections with the phone's own. Setup

OUI-Spy is ESP32 firmware for the XIAO ESP32-S3 with several modes (Detector, Flock-You, Foxhunter, Sky Spy), a buzzer and LED, and a web flasher. OUI-APEX (lukeswitz's fork) runs those engines from one firmware and pairs with its own cross-platform app.

It isn't either/or: RF Sentinel reads OUI-Spy boards over USB, and OUI-SPY App-Controlled boards over Bluetooth, and merges their detections with the phone's own (blue ESP32 badge). The board gives range and radio modes a phone lacks; RF Sentinel adds its rule set, patrol-vehicle grouping, the camera map, alerts and Android Auto.

Wardrive Go alternative: what's the difference with RF Sentinel?

Wardrive Go (GPL-3.0, Android) is a wardriving app: it maps WiFi, Bluetooth and cellular networks, uploads to WiGLE and WDGWars, supports USB monitor-mode WiFi adapters and has channel / spectrum analyzers, Android Auto and a Wear OS companion. It also flags Flock cameras and Axon body cams.

RF Sentinel is built around detection and alerting rather than mapping for upload: sourced signatures with confidence tiers, evidence fusion, patrol-vehicle grouping, follower alerts, Remote ID decoding, fake-cell signs and warnings before mapped cameras. Use Wardrive Go to contribute to WiGLE; use RF Sentinel to be warned while you drive. Some of RF Sentinel's prefix data was cross-checked with Wardrive Go's lists.

Fieldwatch alternative for detecting police and surveillance gear

Fieldwatch (MIT, Android) is a receive-only WiFi and Bluetooth LE observer with customizable views, a filtering engine, an extensible signature library and reports, fully offline. It's a general field-survey tool.

RF Sentinel shares the receive-only, offline approach but is tuned for surveillance and police gear: alert tiers, patrol-vehicle grouping, tracker follow detection, drone and cell analysis, the camera map and Android Auto. Part of RF Sentinel's prefix data was cross-checked with Fieldwatch's list.

SØPHIA vs RF Sentinel: what's the difference?

SØPHIA is a closed-source passive BLE and WiFi recon app for Android sold by DetecX. RF Sentinel does the same kind of passive WiFi and Bluetooth recon for free and open source, with police, ALPR, tracker, drone and fake-cell detection, a camera map and Android Auto. Full comparison: RF Sentinel vs SØPHIA.

Flock-You for Android alternative

Flock-You (Android) is a separate counter-surveillance app with broad multi-protocol ambitions (BLE, WiFi, cellular, GNSS, ultrasonic) and Flipper Zero integration. RF Sentinel stays with what a phone's radios can verify, cites a source for every rule, and connects to ESP32 boards instead of a Flipper. Part of RF Sentinel's prefix data was cross-checked with its list.

Best app to detect surveillance over both Bluetooth and WiFi (instead of AirGuard, BLE Radar, WiFi analyzers)

Many good tools cover one radio or one job:

  • Bluetooth only: AirGuard (AirTag / Find My tracker protection), BLE Radar (MetaRadar) (BLE environment monitoring and custom filters), nRF Connect (a developer's BLE scanner), RF Party (BLE from the DEF CON 31 talk).
  • WiFi only: WiFi analyzer apps show access points and channels but don't identify police or surveillance gear.
  • Cell only: EFF's Rayhunter (IMSI catcher detection on dedicated hardware).
  • Mapping: WiGLE WiFi Wardriving and Wardrive Go map networks for upload.

RF Sentinel puts Bluetooth LE, WiFi and cell analysis in one app, so evidence from different radios can add up (e.g. a router, a radio and a printer moving together = possible police vehicle). A single-radio tool can't make that connection.

Is there an iPhone version?

No. iOS hides Bluetooth MAC addresses and most advertisement fields from apps, offers no WiFi scan API, and doesn't expose cell information, so most of RF Sentinel's detection can't run there. It is Android only.

Install, update, devices

Is RF Sentinel free? Are there ads or in-app purchases?

Free, no ads, no in-app purchases, no subscription, no account. Licensed GPL-3.0.

Is it on Google Play or F-Droid?

Not currently. The signed APK is published on GitHub Releases for every version. See the install guide.

How do I install the APK?
  1. On the phone, open the latest release.
  2. Under Assets, tap the file ending in -release.apk (not "Source code").
  3. Open the downloaded file from the notification or Files → Downloads.
  4. If Android blocks it, tap Settings, enable Allow from this source, go back and tap Install.
  5. Open RF Sentinel. The setup wizard covers theme, region, alerts, camera download radius and permissions.
Is the APK safe?

The full source is public, every release is signed with the same release key (Android refuses an update signed with a different key), and you can build it yourself with ./gradlew assembleDebug. The app requests no contacts, storage, camera or microphone permissions. Play Protect may warn about any app installed outside Google Play; that warning is about the install source, not a scan result.

How do I update?

Install the newer APK over the existing one. Settings, history and watchlist are kept.

Which Android versions and phones are supported?

Android 8.0 (API 26) and newer, on any phone with Bluetooth LE: Pixel, Samsung, Motorola, OnePlus, Xiaomi and others. Hidden-network WiFi details need Android 11+. Bluetooth 5 extended advertising and LE Coded PHY (long range) are used automatically where the chip supports them.

Does it need root?

No. Everything uses public Android APIs.

Why does it ask for location?

Android ties Bluetooth and WiFi scan results to the location permission (up to Android 11 always, and still for WiFi scans and cell info). RF Sentinel also uses GPS for traces, follow detection and camera warnings. Nearby devices (Bluetooth scan) is required; notifications, Display over other apps (bubble) and Nearby devices → connect (only to list your paired car) are optional.

Scanning stops on Samsung / Xiaomi / OnePlus. What do I do?

Some manufacturers kill background apps aggressively. Scanning runs as a foreground service with a notification, but you may still need to set RF Sentinel's battery usage to Unrestricted. Settings has a shortcut to Android's battery optimization screen.

How detection works

How does passive Bluetooth LE detection work?

BLE devices broadcast advertising packets on channels 37, 38 and 39 so other devices can find them. Each packet carries an address, flags and optional fields: device name, 16/128-bit service UUIDs, service data, manufacturer data with a Bluetooth SIG company ID, TX power. RF Sentinel receives these through Android's BluetoothLeScanner and matches every field against its rules. It never connects, so the device never knows it was seen.

What is a MAC vendor prefix (OUI) and why does it matter?

The first 3 bytes of a public MAC address (MA-L), or 3.5 / 4.5 bytes for MA-M / MA-S blocks, are assigned by the IEEE to a company. If a device uses its public address, the prefix reveals the maker, e.g. 00:25:DF is Axon Enterprise. RF Sentinel ships the IEEE registry offline and regional watchlist presets (Global, Canada, US) of law-enforcement and surveillance makers, each prefix re-checked against its registrant. Devices using random private addresses defeat prefix matching, which is why payload signatures and fingerprint linking matter more.

What do weak, probable and strong mean?
TierConfidenceMeaning
Weak (verify)< 50Identifier shared with many unrelated products. Amber, silent by default.
Probable50-79Vendor-specific identifier. Alerts at the default threshold.
Strong80+Payload tag, or a network name the vendor gives its own devices.
What is evidence fusion?

When different rules of the same category match one device, the scores combine by noisy-OR, with each supporting hit discounted by half because rules often share evidence: 1 - (1 - top) × Π(1 - 0.5 × support). Hits under 30 never corroborate, and fusion never exceeds 90. Example: watchlisted address 70 + Zebra serial name 50 → 78.

Devices change their Bluetooth address. How does it keep track?

A structural advert fingerprint survives address rotation: names, service UUIDs, service-data and company-ID payload sizes, TX power and connectable flag. When a new private address appears with the same fingerprint as exactly one device that went silent 1.5-30 s earlier, it inherits that device's matches (at −5), follow history and ignore state. Apple-Continuity-only adverts (phones) are never linked, and two identical candidates means no link.

Can I add my own devices or signatures?

Yes. The watchlist takes exact addresses, vendor prefixes (MA-L / MA-M / MA-S) and name or vendor rules, each with a label and score, plus the regional presets. It exports as JSON. Long-press any device to add it.

I found an unidentified device. How do I report it?

Long-press it → Report unknown device. The report shows exactly what is shared before you send it: vendor prefix, name pattern with serial digits hidden, service and manufacturer IDs. No full address, serial, location or time. Paste it into a GitHub issue or share it on Discord. New signatures need a registry or published source; guesses aren't merged.

Range, accuracy, battery, screen off

How far away can it detect devices?

It depends on the transmitter's power, walls, bodies and your phone's antenna. Bluetooth LE from a body camera is typically heard at tens of metres in the open and much less through a vehicle or building; WiFi access points carry further. Phones with LE Coded PHY hear Bluetooth 5 long-range adverts further. An external ESP32 board with a better antenna extends range. Phone radios are weaker than dedicated hardware, so expect fewer catches than purpose-built scanners.

Can it tell the distance or direction of a device?

Distance is estimated from RSSI and is often off by 2-3×. The radar view places stronger signals closer to the centre, but the angle is not a direction: a phone can't measure angle of arrival. To find a device, use Locate mode and walk: it beeps faster as the signal grows.

Why does WiFi only update every 30 seconds?

Android throttles apps to about four WiFi scans per two minutes. The default 30-second interval stays within that limit. Bluetooth LE is continuous.

Why doesn't it see WiFi phones, laptops or cameras connected to a network?

Android's WiFi scan only lists access points (beacons and probe responses). Client devices, such as an in-car laptop connected to the car's router, need monitor mode, which Android doesn't offer without root and a supported chip. An ESP32 board can partly fill that gap.

Does it work with the screen off?

Partly. Android pauses app Bluetooth scanning while the screen is off, and on some phones (a Pixel in our tests) no Bluetooth results arrive at all until the screen is back on. WiFi, the fake-cell checks, known-camera warnings and ESP32 / USB hardware keep working. Keep the screen on for Bluetooth: the car-mount default "keep screen on while charging" does that while driving. Android Auto warns you when a scan starts with the phone screen off and shows "Bluetooth limited" on its home screen.

How much battery does it use?

Continuous scanning costs battery. Settings → BLE scan mode offers Low latency (default, catches the most), Balanced and Low power, and the WiFi interval is adjustable. Auto-start in the car scans only while you drive.

What can't it detect at all?

Anything that doesn't broadcast BLE or WiFi beacons: cellular-only devices (most non-Flock plate readers, LTE GPS trackers), wired cameras, radios' voice channels, radar and lidar, devices switched off or in a non-advertising state, and WiFi clients. Cameras in OpenStreetMap are covered by the map layer instead.

Android Auto and driving

Is there a police detector app for Android Auto?

RF Sentinel runs in Android Auto: threat headline, the next known camera on your way, recent alerts, Start/Stop and mute on the home screen, device lists with the phone's filters, cell towers, hardware status, a live OpenStreetMap map with devices, cameras and drones, OSM navigation with spoken turns, and alerts as heads-up cards through the car speakers with Mute 30 min and Ignore buttons. See Android Auto.

RF Sentinel doesn't show up in Android Auto. How do I fix it?

In a real car, Android Auto only shows apps built with Google's Car App Library, like RF Sentinel, when they were installed from a trusted store (Google Play or ONE store). Its Unknown sources developer setting covers media and messaging apps, not these (Google's testing guide), so the GitHub APK's car screens can't appear there; they run on Google's Desktop Head Unit emulator. It still works in the car through the app types that setting covers: in Android Auto settings tap Version ten times, open ⋮ → Developer settings, enable Unknown sources and reconnect. RF Sentinel then shows a media-style screen (threats, cameras, alerts, status, with Scan and Mute buttons), and each alert arrives as a message you can have read aloud; reply "mute" to silence alerts for 30 minutes. Tick it under Customize launcher if it's missing.

Can I use it with Waze or Google Maps on screen?

Yes. The floating threat bubble (needs Display over other apps) sits over any app: green when clear, orange for probable, red for strong or a follower, with the count. Tap to open, drag to move. Spoken alerts briefly lower other audio.

Can it start by itself when I get in the car?

Yes. Settings → Start scanning automatically in the car: pick your car's Bluetooth. Scanning starts when the phone connects to it or to Android Auto, and stops when you leave if the car started it.

ESP32, OUI-Spy, GhostESP, Marauder

Can I use an ESP32 with RF Sentinel?

Yes. Plug a board running OUI-Spy , GhostESP or ESP32 Marauder into the phone with a USB OTG cable while scanning, or pair an OUI-SPY board running the Bluetooth (App-Controlled) firmware wirelessly. Its detections merge into the list with a blue ESP32 badge. Native-USB boards (S2/S3/C3/C5/C6) and CP210x / CH340 bridges work. See ESP32 boards.

How do I pair an OUI-SPY board over Bluetooth?

Start scanning with the board on, then Settings → Pair OUI-SPY board and pick OUI-SPY-xxxx. Pair only there, not in Android's Bluetooth settings: the board keeps no pairing keys, so a phone-side bond breaks the link (the app tells you to tap Forget if it finds one).

Can I use a USB WiFi adapter like the ALFA AWUS036ACS, AWUS1900, AWUS036ACM, AWUS036H or AWUS036NHA with Android?

Yes, with RF Sentinel 2.11+. Plug an RTL8811AU / RTL8821AU adapter (e.g. AWUS036ACS) into the phone with an OTG cable while scanning and allow USB access. RF Sentinel drives it directly over Android's USB host API (no root), in receive-only monitor mode on the 2.4 GHz channels: no 30-second scan limit, longer range, and it hears client devices (laptops, phones, cameras on a network) that a phone's WiFi scan can't. The driver is ported from Wardrive Go (GPL-3.0). Since 2.12, RTL8812BU / RTL8822BU adapters (TP-Link Archer T3U, ASUS USB-AC53/AC55/AC58, Edimax EW-7822U*, generic AC1200 dongles) work too, on 2.4 and 5 GHz, with a driver ported from OpenIPC devourer (GPL-2.0); single-antenna dongles are handled automatically. With an adapter plugged in, RF Sentinel can also list the WiFi names nearby devices ask for (Menu → Requested networks) and alert when a watched name is asked for. Since 2.13, RTL8814AU (AWUS1900) and MT7612U (AWUS036ACM) adapters work on 2.4 and 5 GHz, RTL8187 (AWUS036H) and RT3070 (AWUS036NH / NEH) on 2.4 GHz, and AR9271 (AWUS036NHA) experimentally; if one misbehaves, Settings → Export USB WiFi adapter log makes a text file to send. RTL8812AU and RTL8811CU / 8821CU are recognised but not supported yet.

How do I see the cell towers my Android phone is connected to?

RF Sentinel's Tools button → Cell towers lists the serving cell and its neighbours live (2G-5G, operator, MCC-MNC, area code, cell ID, PCI, channel, signal) and every tower seen while scanning. They also appear in the live list (Cells filter) and optionally on the map at the spot where the signal was strongest. An optional alert says when the serving tower changes. No online lookup is made, so nothing reveals where you are.

Is there a WiFi channel and spectrum analyzer in RF Sentinel?

Yes: Tools → WiFi channels (networks and strongest signal per channel) and WiFi spectrum (each network as a curve across its channel width) for 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz, from the phone's own scans.

Does it work with Flipper Zero or a WiFi Pineapple?

A Flipper Zero with an ESP32 board running Marauder (for example the BFFB) works since 2.11.1: plug the Flipper into the phone and open GPIO → USB-UART Bridge. A WiFi Pineapple isn't supported. Other supported hardware: ESP32 boards running OUI-Spy, GhostESP or Marauder, and RTL8811AU / 8821AU or RTL8812BU / 8822BU USB WiFi adapters.

Can I use ESP32 Marauder or an ESP32-C5 board for 5 GHz WiFi on Android?

Yes, with RF Sentinel 2.11.1+. Plug a board running ESP32 Marauder into the phone with a USB OTG cable while scanning. RF Sentinel runs only its passive beacon and probe sniffers and adds the networks and client devices to the list. On ESP32-C5 boards (Marauder v8, BFFB v2, LilyGo T-Dongle C5, C5 DevKit) that includes 5 GHz. Keep Channel Hop on in Marauder's settings. See ESP32 boards.

Privacy, safety and legality

Does RF Sentinel transmit or jam anything?

No. It never transmits, jams, spoofs or connects to the devices it detects. (The phone's own WiFi scan sends standard probe requests like any phone looking for networks, and an OUI-SPY board in relay mode does the same; that can be turned off.)

Can police or anyone tell I'm running it?

The app itself sends nothing to the devices it scans. Your phone, like every phone, may still advertise its own Bluetooth and probe for WiFi networks independently of RF Sentinel.

What data does it collect?

None leaves the phone. No accounts, no analytics, no crash reporting. Detections are stored in a local database excluded from cloud backup. Network requests go only to OpenStreetMap services (map tiles, Overpass camera data from overpass-api.de or its mirrors, DeFlock's region files) and, in Android Auto navigation, Nominatim and OSRM. They see your IP and the area or route, never your scans.

Is it legal to detect police equipment?

RF Sentinel only receives signals that devices broadcast publicly by design, and it doesn't decode private communications. Passive reception of Bluetooth and WiFi advertisements is generally lawful, but rules differ by country and state, and some places restrict use in vehicles or of radar detectors. Check your local laws. This is not legal advice.

What is it meant for?

Awareness, research and personal privacy: knowing which surveillance devices are around you, finding trackers, documenting plate-reader deployments. A detection says a device with a given signature is nearby; it isn't proof of who is present or what they're doing.

Can I export detections?

Yes: current scan as CSV, JSON or KML; all devices ever seen as CSV or JSON; the match log as CSV, JSON, GPX or KML; GPS traces with their devices as GPX, KML, GeoJSON or CSV; the watchlist as JSON; or everything in one JSON file.
