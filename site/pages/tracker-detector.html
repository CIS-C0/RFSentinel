AirTag & tracker detection

Bluetooth trackers away from their owner change what they broadcast. RF Sentinel catches that frame, follows the tag through its address rotations, and tells you when it has been travelling with you.

Follow detection

Trackers rotate their Bluetooth address to stay anonymous. RF Sentinel links the new address to the old one by an advert fingerprint (payload sizes, UUIDs, TX power, connectable flag; new address within 1.5-30 s of the old one going silent, exactly one candidate) and carries the time-with-you and GPS path across. A FOLLOWING alert fires once a tracker or flagged device has stayed with you for at least 10 minutes while you moved at least 800 m. Both are adjustable (2-240 minutes).

Finding the tag

  1. Tap the device → Locate. The beep rate rises with signal strength, and it keeps following the tag when the tag changes its address. Open the map and a live read card shows the signal, rough distance and whether you're getting closer.
  2. Walk around the car or bag; RSSI changes fast within the last metre.
  3. Check the signal graph and history on the detail screen to see when it first appeared.

A car following you: tire-pressure sensors

With an RTL-SDR on the phone (Settings → RTL-SDR radio), RF Sentinel decodes the 315 and 433.92 MHz tire-pressure sensors (TPMS) in car wheels, which each send an ID. The same car's sensors heard with you at place after place means a vehicle following you. Your own car's sensors are learned first and never alert. A tire sensor heard together with a flagged Bluetooth device again after a gap, or across 3 places 2 km apart, ties them to one vehicle (kept 30 days, never backed up). Decoders follow rtl_433. See the RTL-SDR guide.

Without an SDR, a flagged device or tracker that keeps turning up at 3 or more places along your way within 2 hours also raises may be following you, even with gaps between.

Your own tags

Long-press your own AirTag or SmartTag and ignore it. The ignore follows it through address changes, so it stays quiet.

Questions

Android already has unknown tracker alerts. Why use this?

Android's alerts are good but run on Google's schedule and thresholds and show little detail. RF Sentinel shows trackers live, with raw evidence, signal history, the path they followed and adjustable thresholds, alongside everything else around you.

Why don't I see AirTags that are with their owners?

Near its owner an AirTag broadcasts a different, shorter frame and isn't a threat to you. RF Sentinel only flags the separated frame, so people's own keys in a café don't alert.

What about LTE GPS trackers?

RF Sentinel can't see them: they report over the cellular network and broadcast no Bluetooth a phone can identify. Inspect the vehicle physically.

How do I detect an AirTag on Android?

Install RF Sentinel and start scanning. It lists AirTags away from their owners as soon as it hears them, alerts when one stays with you, and its Locate beeps faster as you get closer to the tag.

Is Apple's Tracker Detect app enough on Android?

Apple's Tracker Detect scans only when you ask it to, and only for Find My items. RF Sentinel scans in the background and covers AirTags, Samsung SmartTags, Tile, Chipolo and Find Hub tags, with follower alerts and a Locate screen.

Can I find lost Bluetooth earbuds or a lost tag with my phone?

Often, if it still has battery and is advertising. Open it from RF Sentinel's nearby devices list and tap Locate: the beeps speed up as the signal gets stronger, and it keeps following the device when it changes its address.
